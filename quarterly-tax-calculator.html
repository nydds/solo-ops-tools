The two rules that decide whether you owe a penalty

Rule 1 — 90% of this year

Pay at least 90% of your 2026 tax through withholding and estimated payments, spread across the four due dates as income arrives. If your profit comes in higher than you projected, the 90% test is measured against the real figure at filing, not your estimate.

Rule 2 — the safe harbor

Pay 100% of your 2025 total tax — or 110% if your 2025 AGI was over $150,000 — and you are protected no matter how much this year earns. It is the reason a big year does not have to mean a big penalty; it is also the reason a bad year still asks you to pay last year's amount.

The penalty is the smaller of the two requirements: you need 90% of this year's tax or the safe harbor amount, whichever is lower. Underpaying is charged interest-like at the federal short-term rate plus three points, not as a lump fine, so it grows the longer a quarter stays short.

Penalty-avoidance checklist

  1. Pay on the date, not in the month. Late by one day means that installment is short. Pay through IRS Direct Pay or EFTPS — free, and dated the day you schedule it, not the day it clears.
  2. Check the safe harbor first. If paying 100% (or 110%) of your 2025 tax is less than 90% of your projected 2026 tax, that is all you need to avoid the penalty, and the rest can wait until April 2027.
  3. Use withholding late in the year if you fall behind. Withholding is treated as paid evenly through all four quarters, so raising your W-4 withholding in November can still fix an underpaid April — an estimated payment in November cannot.
  4. Uneven income? Say so on Form 2210. If most of your income arrived in one quarter, the annualized income installment method (Schedule AI) recalculates each installment against the income actually earned by then, and can erase a penalty that the flat method would charge.
  5. Stay under $1,000 due. If the tax still owed after withholding is under $1,000, the underpayment penalty does not apply.
  6. Label every payment. Form 1040-ES with your name, SSN, and "2026" — a payment credited to the wrong year is, for penalty purposes, not made.
  7. Check your state separately. Most states with an income tax run their own quarterly schedule and their own safe harbor; paying the IRS on time says nothing about them.
  8. Overpaid in April? You can elect on the return to apply the overpayment to 2027's first installment instead of taking a refund — useful if your income keeps climbing.

How the estimate is calculated

  1. Projected 2026 tax = self-employment tax (15.3% of 92.35% of profit, Social Security capped at the $184,500 wage base, plus the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax above $200,000 single / $250,000 joint) + federal income tax on profit after half the SE tax, the 2026 standard deduction, and the §199A deduction of 20% of qualified business income.
  2. Required annual payment = the lower of 90% of the projection and the safe harbor (100% or 110% of your 2025 total tax).
  3. Each payment = (required annual payment − expected withholding) ÷ 4. Withholding is deemed paid evenly across the year, which is why it is subtracted before the split.
  4. Full coverage each = (projected tax − withholding) ÷ 4 — what you would pay to owe nothing in April instead of just avoiding the penalty.

Federal only. State estimates, the net investment income tax, capital gains at their own rates, and the §199A wage limitation are not modeled. The 1099 Tax Estimator on this site leaves §199A out, so its total can read higher than the projection here.

When the four dates are not enough

Four equal payments are the simple path, not the required one. A freelancer whose income lands in two big projects can pay 30% in September and almost nothing in April, and the annualized income installment method on Form 2210 will treat that as timely — but only if the income really arrived that way, and only if you file Schedule AI with the return.

The other common shape is a year that starts slow and ends strong. Under the flat method you are expected to have paid a quarter of the year's tax by April 15 even if you had earned nothing by then. That is when people switch to withholding, or to the annualized method, and stop paying a penalty on money they had not yet made.

If your profit passes the Social Security wage base, the fourth quarter is usually the cheapest one: the 12.4% Social Security portion is already fully paid and only the 2.9% Medicare portion plus income tax continues.

Questions this calculator gets

Do I have to make estimated payments at all?

Only if you expect to owe $1,000 or more after withholding. Below that, the penalty does not apply — but the tax is still due by April 15, 2027, and paying late in one lump means no penalty, not no tax.

What counts as my 2025 total tax for the safe harbor?

Form 1040, line 24 of your 2025 return: income tax plus self-employment tax, before subtracting withholding and estimated payments. Do not use your balance due — that is the number after withholding, and using it understates the safe harbor by a lot.

My income jumped this year. Should I pay 110%?

If you can cover it, the 110% safe harbor is the cheapest way to be certain no penalty applies, because it is fixed by last year's return. What it does not do is cover this year's actual bill — the difference is still due in April 2027, which is what the "full coverage each" column shows.

Does an S-corp change the schedule?

The dates do not change, but the amount does: payroll tax comes out of your paychecks each month instead of being estimated, and you estimate only on the salary plus distributions. See the S-Corp vs LLC calculator for what the election does to the total.
