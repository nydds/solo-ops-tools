## Federal income tax (IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, published Oct 9, 2025)
- Standard deduction: Single $16,100 · Married filing jointly $32,200 · Head of household $24,150 · Married filing separately $16,100.
- Single brackets (taxable income): 10% $0–$12,400 · 12% $12,400–$50,400 · 22% $50,400–$105,700 · 24% $105,700–$201,775 · 32% $201,775–$256,225 · 35% $256,225–$640,600 · 37% over $640,600.
- MFJ brackets (taxable income): 10% $0–$24,800 · 12% $24,800–$100,800 · 22% $100,800–$211,400 · 24% $211,400–$403,550 · 32% $403,550–$512,450 · 35% $512,450–$768,700 · 37% over $768,700.
- Cross-checked against IRS news release IR-2025-103 and multiple independent summaries of Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (all agree).

## Self-employment tax (IRS / SSA)
- SE tax rate 15.3% = Social Security 12.4% + Medicare 2.9% (Schedule SE).
- Taxable base = 92.35% of net self-employment earnings.
- 2026 Social Security wage base (contribution and benefit base): $184,500 — Social Security Administration, announced Oct 24, 2025. Max Social Security portion for a self-employed person: $22,878 (12.4% × $184,500). Medicare has no wage cap.
- Additional Medicare tax 0.9% on earnings over $200,000 (single) / $250,000 (married filing jointly) — IRS.
- One-half of SE tax is deductible from income when computing federal income tax (above-the-line deduction).

## Estimated tax payments (IRS Form 1040-ES, 2026)
- Due dates for tax year 2026: April 15, 2026 · June 15, 2026 · September 15, 2026 · January 15, 2027.
- You may skip the January 15, 2027 payment if you file your 2026 return and pay the full balance by February 1, 2027.
- Required annual payment = the lesser of 90% of the current year's tax or the prior-year safe harbor (IRC §6654(d)).
- Safe harbor: 100% of the prior year's total tax, or 110% of it if the prior year's AGI exceeded $150,000 ($75,000 married filing separately) — IRC §6654(d)(1)(C).
- No underpayment penalty when the tax remaining after withholding is under $1,000 (IRC §6654(e)(1)).
- Withholding is treated as paid ratably over the year regardless of when it was withheld (IRC §6654(g)(1)), which is why raising W-4 withholding late in the year can cure an earlier shortfall.
- Uneven income: the annualized income installment method (Form 2210, Schedule AI) recalculates each installment on income actually received by that date.
- Source pages: IRS Form 1040-ES (2026) and Instructions for Form 2210; IRS "Estimated taxes" (irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax).

## S corporation vs sole proprietor / single-member LLC (2026)
- Payroll (FICA) on S-corp wages: employee 6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare; employer the same; Social Security applies up to the $184,500 wage base, Medicare has no cap. The employer half is a deduction of the company; the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax has no employer match.
- FUTA: 0.6% net of the full state credit on the first $7,000 of wages per employee — $42 per year for a one-owner S corp (IRC §3301; Form 940).
- Reasonable compensation: an S-corp shareholder who works in the business must be paid reasonable wages before distributions (IRC §1361; IRS "S corporation compensation and medical insurance issues"); there is no statutory percentage, and the IRS may reclassify distributions as wages.
- Qualified business income (§199A): 20% of QBI, and S-corp wages are not QBI, so the deduction applies only to the K-1 distribution. 2026 threshold $201,750 (all other returns) / $403,500 (married filing jointly), phase-in range top $276,750 / $553,500 — IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, §4.26. OBBBA (P.L. 119-21) made §199A permanent and added, for tax years beginning after 2025, a $400 minimum deduction and a $1,000 QBI eligibility floor (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, §3.12).
- Form 2553 (S election) due date: generally 2 months and 15 days after the start of the tax year the election is to take effect (March 15 for a calendar-year election), or 2 months and 15 days after entity formation.
- State S-corp costs modeled:
  - California: S corporations pay the greater of the $800 minimum franchise tax or 1.5% of California net income (FTB Form 100S booklet, 2025); a California LLC already pays the $800 annual minimum, so only the excess is counted.
  - New York: Form CT-3-S fixed dollar minimum tax by New York receipts — $25 (≤$100,000), $50 (≤$250,000), $175 (≤$500,000), $300 (≤$1,000,000), $1,000 (≤$5,000,000), $3,000 (≤$25,000,000), $4,500 (over) — from CT-3-S-I; the MTA surcharge does not apply to a New York S corporation (tax.ny.gov, Article 9-A S corporations).
  - New Jersey: Corporation Business Tax statutory minimum by New Jersey gross receipts — $375 (<$100,000), $562 (<$250,000), $750 (<$500,000), $1,125 (<$1,000,000), $1,500 (≥$1,000,000) — NJ CBT-100S instructions, Schedule J.
  - Florida: no personal income tax; the 5.5% corporate income/franchise tax applies to an S corporation only if it pays federal income tax on Form 1120S line 23c (Florida Dept. of Revenue, Corporate Income Tax).
- Modeled against an independent worked example (single filer, $100,000 profit, $60,000 salary, $2,000 costs): this site's figures are $22,365 sole prop vs $21,465 S corp, a $900 saving, matching the published example's $22,364 / $21,465 / $934 within rounding.

## LLC state fees (state Secretary of State fee schedules, verified 2026)
- Wyoming: Articles of Organization $100 · Annual report $60 minimum (or $0.0002 per $1 of assets located in Wyoming, whichever is greater) · No franchise tax · No state individual income tax.
- Delaware: Certificate of Formation $90 · Annual franchise tax $300/year (flat for LLCs).
- Florida: Articles of Organization $125 · Annual report $138.75/year.
- New York: Articles of Organization $200 · Biennial Statement $9 every 2 years · Certificate of Publication $50 · Mandatory newspaper publication (LLC Law § 206): two county-designated newspapers, once a week for six successive weeks, within 120 days — newspaper charges are set by the newspapers, not the state (roughly $200 in low-cost upstate counties to $1,000–$1,900+ in Manhattan) · Annual LLC filing fee Form IT-204-LL on prior-year NY-source gross income: $25 (≤$100,000), $50 ($100,001–$250,000), $175 ($250,001–$500,000), $500 ($500,001–$1,000,000), $1,500 ($1,000,001–$5,000,000), $3,000 ($5,000,001–$25,000,000), $4,500 (over $25,000,000); disregarded single-member LLCs pay a flat $25. No franchise tax for LLCs.

## Notes / limits (what this site does NOT do)
- No state income tax is included in any calculator (federal only), apart from the S-corp-specific state minimums listed above.
- No Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction is applied on the 1099 Tax Estimator; the S-corp and quarterly calculators do apply 20% of QBI, but not the wage/UBIA limitation or the SSTB phase-out above the 2026 threshold.
- The S-corp comparison uses net profit as a stand-in for gross receipts when a state charges its minimum fee by receipts, and does not model city-level taxes (e.g. New York City's General Corporation Tax).
- The quarterly calculator plans four equal installments; the real penalty is computed installment by installment on Form 2210, and the annualized income method is not calculated for you.
- NY publication costs are estimates by county tier, not official quotes; the state does not set newspaper rates.
- Registered-agent fees are not included (they are third-party services, not state fees).
- All results are estimates for planning only — not tax or legal advice.
